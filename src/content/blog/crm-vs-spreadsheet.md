Spreadsheets are the default CRM for a lot of accounting firms — not because anyone chose them, but because nobody chose anything else. A tab gets started to track a few new clients, and five years later it's the backbone of how the firm runs, with quirks and workarounds only one or two people fully understand.

The appeal is obvious: no cost, no setup, no learning curve. But the real cost of a spreadsheet doesn't show up on a bill. It shows up in three quieter ways.

## 1. Information lives in people, not in the system

When client history, preferences, and status live in someone's memory or a scattered set of notes, the firm's institutional knowledge walks out the door the moment that person is out sick, on vacation, or leaves. A CRM makes that knowledge durable — anyone on the team can see where a relationship stands without asking around.

## 2. Nothing happens automatically

A spreadsheet can hold data, but it can't act on it. It won't remind anyone that a lead hasn't been contacted in a week, send a follow-up email, or flag a client whose engagement is coming up for renewal. Every one of those actions depends entirely on someone remembering to check — which, during busy season, is exactly when it stops happening.

## 3. There's no real reporting

"How many leads did we get this quarter, and how many became clients?" is a simple question that a spreadsheet-based firm often can't answer without hours of manual reconciliation. Without that visibility, marketing spend and time end up allocated on instinct rather than evidence.

## What a CRM changes

A CRM doesn't just organize the same information more neatly — it turns the firm's client and lead data into something the system can act on: automatic follow-up sequences, status tracking that updates itself, and reporting that's accurate because it's built into how work already happens, not bolted on afterward.

The switch isn't about the tool for its own sake. It's about removing the number of things that have to go right, manually, for a lead not to fall through the cracks.
